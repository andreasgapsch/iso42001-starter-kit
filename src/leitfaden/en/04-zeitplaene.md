# 4 Schedules

Two schedules, one per path. Both end with **stage 1 readiness**: all documents are approved, the controls are running, and an internal audit and a management review have taken place. This is not the certificate.

**A frank assessment.** Both schedules apply to the scope described in chapter 5 (a small, clearly defined scope, at most one in-house development, no high-risk AI) and rest on these assumptions:

- **Time commitment:** the AIMS lead spends about 40 to 50 per cent of their working time on the project, on both paths. They carry about 60 per cent of the person-days in chapter 5. Top management, IT, business units and, with an ISMS, the ISMS team provide the rest in addition. At about 20 per cent, expect about 9 months without an ISMS and about 4 to 5 months with an ISMS.
- **Approvals:** top management approves within a week, following an approval calendar (below).
- **Extended scope:** with an ISMS but several in-house developments, AI in your own product, a high-risk system under the EU AI Act or several companies, expect **about 6 months** to stage 1 readiness and about 45 to 55 PD. Without an ISMS such a scope takes correspondingly longer. The example Muster Maschinenbau AG has this extended scope.

Consultants and certification bodies quote a realistic **6 to 12 months with an ISMS** and **12 to 18 months without an ISMS** up to the certificate. The difference arises after stage 1: waiting for audit dates, several months of operating evidence, stage 2, corrections and the certification decision.

## Approval calendar for top management

On both paths top management approves seven times. Fix the meetings in week 1 and bundle the approvals around them. During the project, top management should meet every two weeks or approve by circulation. With a monthly agenda item, expect 4 to 6 months instead of 90 days.

| Week with ISMS | Week without ISMS | What top management approves | Templates |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | project mandate; without an ISMS also the short version of document control | D-00, D-26 |
| 4 | 6 | AI policy, scope, roles | D-02, D-03, D-04 |
| 5 | 10 | risk criteria and impact assessment procedure | D-06, D-08 |
| 7 | 12 | full impact assessments and restricted use | D-09 |
| 8 | 14 | treatment plan, residual risks, statement of applicability | D-07, D-11 |
| 10 | 18 | policies, procedures, process descriptions, objectives | D-05, D-12 to D-17, D-26, D-27 |
| 12 | 22 | management review minutes | D-21 |

Without a slot of their own in the calendar: context and parties (D-01) and the AI inventory (D-10) are approved by the AIMS lead under the approval rules in D-26 before top management approves scope and policy. The AIMS lead keeps the audit programme and audit report (D-20) and the register of nonconformities (D-22); top management receives them as input to the management review (D-21).

## After stage 1

After the stage 1 audit, plan with these reference values from practice:

- **Between stage 1 and stage 2** there are usually a few weeks to a few months. During this time you address the points raised in stage 1 and keep collecting operating evidence.
- **Stage 2** checks on site or by video whether the AIMS runs as described. Major nonconformities must be resolved before the certificate is issued; for minor nonconformities an action plan is usually enough.
- **The certification decision** and issue of the certificate take two to six weeks at many bodies.

Booking the body early (quotations in week 4 with an ISMS, in weeks 7 to 8 without) and running operations from week 8 shortens the overall time the most.
