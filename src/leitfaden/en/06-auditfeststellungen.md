# 6 The five most frequent audit findings and how to avoid them

Consultants and certification bodies keep reporting the following five findings when they describe early audits under ISO/IEC 42001 (Glocert, 4C Consulting, Drel, supported by DQS and ISO; sources in the appendix). They are **observations from practice, not statistics**. They match the experience from audits of other management systems: a document is rarely missing altogether. What is usually missing is evidence that it is lived.

The ‘check rule’ notes refer to the checks in the AIMS Manager. They report errors (F) and notes (H) before an auditor does.

## 1 Incomplete AI inventory and unclear scope

**What auditors find.** AI in purchased software, SaaS services and shadow AI are missing from the inventory. The scope lists systems that no longer exist and leaves out new ones.

**How to avoid it.** Build the inventory from several sources: questionnaire, licence lists, purchasing, browser extensions, logs. Ask about functions (‘summarizes’, ‘suggests’, ‘recognizes’), not about the word AI. Every system has a system owner. Approving a new tool (D-15) automatically leads to an inventory entry.

**In the kit.** D-10 with questionnaire, D-02, D-15. Check rules F01 (no system owner), H02 (shadow AI under review for more than 30 days).

## 2 Generic risk assessment

**What auditors find.** The same five risks appear for every system. System-specific risks are missing, as are the acceptance of residual risks with name and date and the triggers for a reassessment. Nobody has touched the register since it was created.

**How to avoid it.** Assess each system on its own, with the people who use it. Ask: what happens if the system is wrong, and who notices? Record who accepts the residual risk and which events trigger a new assessment.

**In the kit.** D-06, D-07. Check rules F02, F08, F09, F19, H20, H21, H23.

## 3 Missing impact assessment and unjustified exclusions

**What auditors find.** For systems that affect people, the impact assessment is missing or has not been approved. In the statement of applicability, controls are excluded without a reason, or with the reason ‘already in place’.

**How to avoid it.** Carry out the screening for every system. Justify every exclusion by context, role or risk, for example: ‘We do not develop AI systems; life cycle controls for development do not apply.’ Whatever is in place is applicable and implemented. Keep to the order: role, risks, impact assessment, then approval of the system and the statement of applicability. If you change risks or actions afterwards, approve the treatment plan and the statement of applicability again.

**In the kit.** D-08, D-09, D-11. Check rules F03 to F06, F26, H04, H12, H17 to H19, H22.

## 4 Gap between policy and practice

**What auditors find.** The policies are well written, but there are no test reports, no logs, no approvals of new systems and no evidence of human oversight or monitoring. Controls are marked ‘implemented’ without evidence.

**How to avoid it.** For every control in D-11, define which evidence daily work produces and where it is kept. Use existing tools (ticket system, supplier logs) instead of new forms. Start operations early, so that several weeks of records exist by the time of the audit.

**In the kit.** D-11, D-12, D-14. Check rules F07, H05, H08, H09.

## 5 Weak internal audit, thin management review, corrections without root cause

**What auditors find.** The internal audit does not cover the AI topics, or it is carried out by people who audit their own area. The management review does not deal with all required inputs, or it is little more than an attendance list. Corrective actions fix the symptom, without root cause analysis and without an effectiveness check.

**How to avoid it.** Audit with the checklist in D-20 and rotate auditors across areas. Use the agenda in D-21, which contains all inputs, and record the decisions. In D-22, record the cause of each finding and check after a few weeks whether the action works.

**In the kit.** D-20, D-21, D-22. Check rules F15 to F17, H11.
