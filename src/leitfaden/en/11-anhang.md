## B Glossary in brief

| Term | Meaning in the kit |
|---|---|
| AIMS | AI management system under ISO/IEC 42001 |
| ISMS | information security management system under ISO/IEC 27001 |
| AI system | system that derives outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions from inputs; the AI Act has its own, similar definition in Art. 3 |
| Shadow AI | AI tool that employees use without approval |
| SaaS AI | AI as a cloud service or as a feature in purchased software |
| Provider, deployer | roles under the EU AI Act: who makes an AI system available under its own name, and who uses it professionally |
| Statement of applicability (SoA) | list of all Annex A controls with decision, justification, status and evidence |
| Control | measure for treating a risk; Annex A of ISO/IEC 42001 lists 38 in 9 groups |
| Impact assessment | structured review of what an AI system means for the people it touches, for specific groups and for society (clause 6.1.4) |
| Screening (triage) | first page of D-09; decides whether a full impact assessment is needed |
| Residual risk | risk that remains after treatment and is accepted by the responsible manager |
| Human oversight | competent people monitor an AI system and can intervene |
| Supplementary sheet | sections that you add to an existing ISMS document on the path with an ISMS (ID `D-xx-EB`) |
| Interested party | person or organization that is affected by the use of AI or influences it |
| Management review | regular review of the AIMS by top management (clause 9.3) |
| Nonconformity | requirement not met; calls for correction, root cause analysis and an effectiveness check |
| Stage 1, stage 2 | the two phases of the certification audit: readiness and documentation, then effectiveness |
| AI literacy | knowledge and skills to use AI competently (AI Act Art. 4) |
| High-risk AI | AI system under Art. 6 of the AI Act, for example in recruitment or as a safety component |
| Generative AI | AI that produces text, images, code or audio |
| Agentic AI (agent) | AI that does not just answer but carries out steps itself with tools and access, for example sends emails or changes data |
| Prompt injection | attack with hidden or smuggled instructions that a language model follows as a command, for example from an email or a document |
| Trustworthiness characteristic | property of trustworthy AI in the NIST AI RMF, for example valid and reliable, safe, fair (chapter 10) |
| Current and target profile | current and intended status per subcategory of the NIST AI RMF (N-01) |
| Harmonised standard | European standard whose reference is published in the Official Journal of the EU; complying with it gives a presumption of meeting the requirements of the AI Act it covers |
| PD | person-day of eight hours |

## F Questions for the board of directors and executive management

The board of directors is responsible for the overall management of the company and the supervision of the persons entrusted with management (Art. 716a para. 1 items 1 and 5 CO; in a limited liability company, the managing directors under Art. 810 para. 2 CO). This includes the use of AI, which changes decisions, processes and liability risks. With these ten questions you can check in one meeting whether the company has AI under control. The kit template that provides the answer is given in brackets.

1. **Overview.** Do we know all AI systems, including AI in purchased software and shadow AI, and does each have a system owner? (D-10)
2. **Agentic AI.** Which systems send emails, trigger bookings or change data on their own, and who can stop them at once? (D-10, D-12)
3. **Data.** Which confidential and personal data flow to AI providers, and does the contract settle what they may do with it? (D-15, D-16)
4. **Limits.** What is never allowed here, and what needs approval by executive management? (D-03)
5. **Decision rights.** Who introduces an AI system, who accepts residual risks, and who may stop or retire a system? (D-04)
6. **Residual risks.** Which residual risks have we knowingly accepted, and until when do these decisions apply? (D-07)
7. **Traceability.** Could we explain a wrong decision by an AI to those affected, customers or an authority, and can those affected appeal? (D-12, D-14)
8. **Human review.** Do the people who review AI results have enough time, knowledge and the right to say no, or do they just wave them through? (D-04, D-17)
9. **Effect.** How do we notice that a system no longer helps or causes harm, and do we learn about it in time? (D-05, D-14, D-21)
10. **Law and next step.** What role do we have under the EU AI Act and data protection law, and what do we settle before the next expansion of AI? (D-24, D-09)

**Good answers** are short and backed by evidence: a list, minutes, a date. Where an answer is missing, give the AIMS lead a task with a deadline. Bring the questions to the management review (D-21) once a year and to the meeting for every larger AI project.

## G Your own edition of the standard in the AIMS Manager

The kit contains no text of the standard. If your company has purchased ISO/IEC 42001 or ISO/IEC 27001 (also SN EN or DIN EN, German or English), load the PDF under **Settings, Texts from your edition of the standard**. First you confirm that you hold a valid licence. The AIMS Manager reads the text in the browser, shows ‘found’ or ‘not found’ for each clause and control and lets you correct individual entries. The app then shows the titles and requirement texts of the standard instead of the kit's own short titles. The text does not leave the computer, is kept in the browser separately from the project and never goes into the project file; ‘Delete texts of the standard’ removes it. Generated documents only include it after the second confirmation ‘Our licence permits inclusion in internal documents’, with a source note. Check your licence: many permit internal use but not disclosure to third parties.

## H What the kit does not decide

The kit presents the following legal questions but does not answer them: they are open or rest on an interpretation by the kit. Before a decision, pass the questions that affect your company to your legal adviser (legal department or lawyer) and record the answer with its date in E-09. The AIMS Manager shows the status of each item with source and review date in the EU AI Act module; this guide reflects the status of 07/11/2026. Not legal advice.
