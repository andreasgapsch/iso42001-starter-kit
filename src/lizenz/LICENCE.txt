6. HOW TO ATTRIBUTE US CORRECTLY
--------------------------------
Wording of the attribution:
  ISO/IEC 42001 Starter Kit by Andreas Gapsch, KMU-CIO, kmu-cio.ch

When you share the kit or parts of it, CC BY 4.0 requires: the attribution,
a reference to the licence with a link and, for modified versions, a note
that you made changes and what they are. A link to the licence is
sufficient: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

Your own documents (for example your approved AI policy or your completed
risk register) need no attribution. The additional permission covers this.

Example 1: sharing within your company
  You place the kit or blank templates from it on your intranet. Keep the
  folder LIZENZ_LICENCE with the kit and write on the intranet page:
    ‘ISO/IEC 42001 Starter Kit by Andreas Gapsch, KMU-CIO, kmu-cio.ch,
    licence CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)’

Example 2: training
  You use content from the guide or templates in slides or handouts. On the
  title or source slide:
    ‘Content partly from: ISO/IEC 42001 Starter Kit by Andreas Gapsch,
    KMU-CIO, kmu-cio.ch, licence CC BY 4.0
    (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), shortened and adapted.’

Example 3: consulting engagement
  Documents that you create for your client need no attribution. If you
  also hand over the kit or blank templates to the client, add this note to
  the handover or the report:
    ‘Working aid: ISO/IEC 42001 Starter Kit by Andreas Gapsch, KMU-CIO,
    kmu-cio.ch, licence CC BY 4.0
    (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)’

Example 4: publishing an adapted version
  You publish your own version, for example for a particular industry. At
  the beginning of the version and in its licence file:
    ‘This version is based on the ISO/IEC 42001 Starter Kit by Andreas
    Gapsch, KMU-CIO, kmu-cio.ch, licence CC BY 4.0
    (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Modified by [name] in
    [month year]: [changes in one sentence]. This version has not been
    reviewed or endorsed by KMU-CIO.’
  Give your version its own name without ‘KMU-CIO’. For software code, keep
  the copyright notice and the text of the MIT License.
